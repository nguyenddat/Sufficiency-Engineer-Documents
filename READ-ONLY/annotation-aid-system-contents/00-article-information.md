Grenoble Alpes, CNRS, Grenoble INP, LIG, Grenoble, 38000, France ## Article information Keywords: smart buildings occupant behavior data annotation residential energy human–AI interaction first-order logic large language models ## Abstract Smart buildings can observe physical consequences of occupant behavior, but they rarely capture the semantic reasons behind those actions. This paper proposes a smart annotation-aiding framework for residential energy and water use, in which occupant-defined inquiries drive the acquisition of sensor evidence and human-provided semantic annotations. Experiments are formalized as inquiry-centered observation processes, and annotations are treated as reusable knowledge rather than disposable training labels. The framework combines inquiry-guided data collection, interactive label assignment, and quality control, and uses first-order logic as a shared representation between sensor-derived facts, symbolic reasoning, and large language models. The approach is illustrated through residential activity use cases and an implementation-oriented evaluation of interactive annotation workflows. The results support annotation-aided sensing as a practical pathway toward more explainable, occupant-centric, and energy-aware building intelligence.
