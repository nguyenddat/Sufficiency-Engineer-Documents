Evaluating the added value of the characterization
of fact : study cases

Annotations for the characterization of facts were recovered ma-
nually in real experiments situations in 2 sites. In this chapter, the
annotations are presented and analysed. The experience of annota-
tion and the outcome of the process is also evaluated from interviews
carried out to the inhabitants.

### 8.1 Characterized facts using semantic annotations

#### 8.1.1 Annotations from two sites : "kappa" and "gamma"

In this part, the objective is to study recovered information as it was proposed in chapter
2 with the characterization of the facts using the semantic annotations (5WH+performance) in
real experiments. Two types of experiment’s questions were evaluated : questions oriented to the
evaluation of the performance and questions oriented to activity/context impact. In the first case,
the inhabitants want to perform an evaluation of the satisfaction of their intention. In the second
case they evaluate the energy consumption resulting of activities or contexts where one or multiple
appliances can be involved. As it was explained in 3.3
The studies were carried out in two different sites. The first site, a single storey apartment
of 25m2 , let’s call it "kappa", it is inhabited by a couple. This site is located in the south of
France in the Provence-Alpes-Côte-de-Azur, region. A thermometer and a sensor to measure po-
wer consumption were installed. Two experiments were carried out responding to the next ques-
tions oriented to the performance evaluation :
1. Does the room temperature drops when I open the window ? (Includes performance evalua-
tion). This experiment was considered interesting given the high temperatures reached in the
summer. Also we could observe the process of evaluation of the performance. Inhabitants
consider this information as a trigger to make changes of behaviour ?
2. How much energy do I consume when I use the washing machine in different wash programs
and in different contexts ? (Includes performance evaluation) This experiment was chosen
given that the power plugs sensors does not provide information of the specific programs
when using the washing machine. It allowed us to observe if there are actually differences in
the energy consumption, if inhabitants use different washing programs and if knowing this
information incites to behaviour changes after evaluating the satisfaction of the washing
cycle vs the energy consumed.
The second site called gamma is located in the Isère Region in France. It is a two storey
apartment of 108 m2 inhabited by a couple. This apartment has around 120 measuring points
and different sensors are installed, such as sensors to measure CO2 levels, power consumption of
appliances, temperature and moments of opening and closing doors and windows.
Four different experiments were carried out in this site to answer to the next questions. Notice
that experiment 1 and 2 are the same as for site "kappa". This will allow to compare the inhabitants
responses for the same questions. However in question 2, the dishwasher is used rather than the
washing machine. Questions 3 and 4 are oriented to the activity impact :
1. Does the room temperature drops when I open the window ? (Includes performance evalua-
tion)
2. How much energy do I consume when I use the dishwasher in different wash programs and
in different contexts ? (Includes performance evaluation)
3. How much energy do I waste when the computer is left on and I’m not working ? This
question could be related to a case of energy waste most probably caused by mistake by the
inhabitants. Does making annotations about situations as such, helps the inhabitant realise
of energy waste cases ?
4. Does the energy consumption of the fridge increase when I return from shopping and put
food inside the fridge ? This question would be difficult to answer by using only sensors
information. It would be interesting to observe what annotations are provided.
The questions were proposed to the inhabitants, given the sensors available and the interest of
the researchers to study specific examples that were considered significant. There was no interac-
tion with an interface in the process of the annotation. The annotations were recorded manually.
A format was provided with each of the semantic annotations to guide the users on what needed

to be annotated. Information about the beginning and the end of the facts were also provided by
the occupants. Notice that annotating manually, could be less comfortable for the occupants, ra-
ther than using a numerical system with an interface that can facilitate the task. We would like to
observe if inhabitants commit to make annotations in this format. It might mean that if the task is
facilitated by using a numerical system, they could involve more easily in the process.
In this section we are interested in studying the next points :
1. Annotating improves understanding of the human behaviour impacts ?
2. Do residents feel interested and involved in making annotations ?
3. Do residents feel interested and involved making changes in their behaviour ?
4. Annotating enables flexibility to be assessed in terms of the changes to be made ?
5. What annotations are provided if inhabitants are told that there is no restriction on what they
can annotate, but they should follow the semantic (5WH+performance) as a guide ?
6. Can the context of the facts be captured by using annotations ?
7. Are there annotations left empty ?
8. Is it interesting for the inhabitants to assess human behaviour and their impact on energy
consumption ?

#### 8.1.2 Performance evaluation experiments

Performance evaluation experiments allow the occupants to register every time that a fact
happens : the intention of the fact, the way that this intention was satisfied (by responding to
proposed characterization questions) and specially, the level of satisfaction of their intention by
fulfilling it with a specific behaviour in a specific context.
We have realised that certain intentions might be fulfilled by different fact options. For instance
if an occupant desires to reduce the temperature, he/she could open the door or the window off the
room or installing an air condition system or a fan. Let’s see some applied examples.

Experiment 1, site "kappa" : Does the room temperature drops when I open the window ?
For the first experiment of site "kappa", it is important to consider that the site is situated in a
location where temperatures in the summer can reach up to 35°C. In this apartment, the room for
the study was a "loggia" (by its name in French). This can be described as a closed balcony with
windows all around. In spring and summer time when temperatures raise, a "green house effect"
is created. They were asked to annotate the characterisation of the facts for a period of 3 weeks.
Often they opened only one window when they intended to cool down the zone. By doing this,
they observed that the temperature lowered down 1°C maximum even when leaving sometimes the
window opened for more than 2 hours. In other cases, they open the small window and a second
window perpendicular to the first. In one occasion, they opened both windows at 8:40am in the
morning, and the temperature in the loggia lowered 3°C in one hour (from 25°C to 22°C. The
external temperature observed in historical temperature data registered around 17°C (Infoclimat,
). Apparently this effect is reached mainly in the mornings. By carrying out an interview, the young
adult living there, stated that sometimes in the morning around 6am to 7am in summer, the balcony
receives direct sun light. When the windows are closed the temperature can raise up to 29°C, but
by opening both windows they observed in another occasion that the temperature reduced 2°C at
7:35am (from 29°C to 27°C), with an external temperature of 22°C (Infoclimat, ).
What is interesting to emphasise from this experiment is that the inhabitants could associate
an intention "cool down the room" with a habit (opening 1 window) and its impact. A habit that
proved to not lower down the temperature, often, in long periods of time from 2 to up to 9 hours.
Then, they could observe the effect of a different modality (opening 2 windows) when having the
same intention and they could observe that in a couple of cases, the temperature could lower up to

3°C in an hour, given that the external temperature was between 17°C and 22°C. The couple has
found out that it might be more efficient to open both windows to lower the temperature and avoid
overheating the zone in the mornings, although two results might not be representative. In the
afternoons, the cooling effect seemed to be less significant, but also in some cases in the morning.
It was also noticed that in several occasions, although the temperature of the zone didn’t actually
decreased, the inhabitants annotated a high degree of satisfaction. Other factors could affect such
as the type of clothes used, and if parallel activities required to be physically passive for example.
The annotation about the place were always the same since it was always carried out in the
same zone. The inhabitants seem to use the space as a "working space" given that "loggia" was
not annotated, but it was annotated "desk".
During the experiment, 5 different reasons to open the windows were annotated "refresh",
"cool down", "interest to hear a strange sound outside", "lower humidity", "dry clothes". In the
How ? question column, it was always written "opening window". In the column of Who ? ques-
tion, (participants implicated) the inhabitants wrote whether it was the woman or the man who
opened the window. In the What ? question (objects implicated column) the inhabitants annotated
the number of windows being opened. This information was important given that in some occa-
sions only one window was used . The last column, where they annotated the performance of the
fact, in the majority of the cases a high satisfaction level was annotated.
In general, it can be concluded from this experiment that given the greater part of the results,
opening one window or two windows might not be enough to lower the temperature, but from
the perspective of the inhabitants it is an action that provides a good satisfaction in terms of com-
fort. Inhabitants annotated mainly the satisfaction based on their feeling rather than based on the
temperature measurements. This was discovered after having carried out the experiment.
The inhabitants are encouraged to continue making observations in the case of the opening of
both windows in the morning to be able to define if this behaviour is, actually efficient to lower
the temperature.
Looking in retrospective, it is interesting to realise that another possible annotation in the
section of objects implicated, the inhabitants could annotate also the type of clothes they wear,
an information that cannot be recovered from sensors and could have an impact in the inhabitants
temperature perception and annotation of performance.

Experiment 1 : site "gamma" Does the room temperature drops when I open the window ?

This experiment was carried out in the living room of site "gamma". The time duration of the
opening of the window was provided by the inhabitant. The maximum duration was of 9 hours
and a half, while the minimum duration was of 2 hours and a half. The intention of opening the
window was defined mainly as "ventilating", although sometimes it was also defined as "too hot"
and "heat". In an interview to the occupants, the person who often opened the window did this
mainly because he wanted to "feel the air" while the other occupant said he prefers to open the
window to "decrease the temperature" or to "take out the cat". Nonetheless, this label was forgotten
to be annotated by the inhabitants. It is important to mention this to show another reason of opening
the window that could impact the internal comfort of the room. It could be seen that annotating the
intention of "feeling the air" or "taking out the cat" were less evident to add, maybe because it was
not linked to a cause that could have direct impact on energy consumption or air quality, such as
"cooling down the room" or "renewing the air". However if the window is left open enough time,
there could be a significant impact.
In the How ? question, the inhabitants answered to leave the window "wide opened" often and
in some cases "semi-opened". This label was provided by the inhabitants after letting them know
that the contact sensor of the window can only detect if the window is open or closed, but it has
no sensor to determine how opened the window is : for instance wide opened or just opened some
centimeters. It is interesting to observe that it is important to explain also the way in which the

sensors work so that the inhabitants know what information might be important to annotate, given
that the sensors cannot detect it.
In the Who ? question (participants involved), two people were mainly declared, but 6 and
3 people happened to be present in an occasion. Differently from the experiment of site 1, the
inhabitants annotated the number of people involved rather than the name. In the objects implied,
the annotation was "window" in all the cases. As for the performance, the occupant annotated in
the moment the satisfaction provided by the feeling of comfort of the inhabitant. Similar to the
case in site "kappa". In the majority of the cases the satisfaction was evaluated with a value of 4,
but in the middle of the month of july, the inhabitant mentioned that if the window appears more
often closed in the last days of the experiment, it is because there was the need to turn on the air
conditioned. The indoor temperatures were high enough and the opening of the window was not
sufficient to cool down the air. One of the inhabitants added that although the satisfaction level
was annotated based on the temperature they felt, they would also want to grade later according to
the temperature measurements in order to grade, based on the degrees that the temperature could
actually diminish (depending on how much the temperature dropped).

Experiment 2 in site "kappa" : How much energy do I consume when I use the washing ma-
chine in different wash programs and in different contexts ?

A sensor to observe the energy consumption of the washing machine was installed and the
measurements were possible to recover thanks to a commercial app called myChacon (Google
Play, ). This app allows to visualize the measurements per hour providing measurements of average
energy consumption per hour in kWh. The inhabitants could then observe the energy impact of the
characterised fact.

The second experiment was carried out in the "kappa" apartment. The facts were characterized
and the performance was, as well, evaluated. As in the previous case, the parametric semantic
annotations were requested to the occupants.
The beginning and end of the fact was annotated, specifying the time of the washing cycle. The
annotation about the Where ? question, place where the washing was carried out was always the
same "bathroom". The Why ? question (intentions) included several different annotations : "bed

sheets", "clothes", The modalities included also a diversity of annotations : "eco-30°C", "eco-
40°C", "60°C" "cotton", "15 min", and "40 min". In the column of the What ? question (objects
implicated), it was always written "washing machine". The performance of the washing cycle was
also evaluated and in the majority of the cases, a high quality was annotated. None the less, in one
occasion the performance was evaluated as 1. An interview was carried out with the inhabitant to
ask about this note. The young couple stated that it was the first time they realised that the 60°C
cycle takes 3h. "Usually we just start the cycle, but we didn’t pay attention to the time. We just let
it work while we do other activities". The energy consumption was higher than in the other type of
cycles. So, for them, it represents a long cycle with a high energy consumption. That is the reason
why they annotated a low performance of the modality. "We’re going to prefer washing at 30°C,
anyway, it’s clean enough", they concluded.
The couple realised as well that each of them washes using a different configuration. The
woman mentioned she often uses 30°C while the man uses mainly 40°C. The woman did not
know that his partner washed mainly at 40°C. This is maybe a good moment to agree if a mode
can be privileged according to the impact and the quality of washing. The woman also shared
that she prefers to do a fast 15 min wash of sport clothes to avoid the sweat odor to stay in the
clothes. For her, doing this has a high quality satisfaction, but for the male, the satisfaction is not
the maximal. Although just for the length of the cycle, it is the washing that consumed less energy.
In one last occasion, the male noted a 3 in the performance when washing at 40°C.
It can be observed that this couple was not aware of the impacts of the washing machine cycle.
The fact of showing the impacts in the app helped to create awareness of unconscious routinized
facts. It seemed that especially the time of the cycle was an important factor for them and an
indicator of the energy consumption that they could later observe thanks to the measurements. The
man mentioned "I’ve realized that I’m used of washing in "eco" mode, but does it really consume
less ? Now I can see the real energy consumption, because it’s measured." In this case, they actually
pointed out that from now on, they would like to better use the cycle at 30°C rather than at 60°C

for the bed sheets, which means they are inclined to perform a more sober behaviour. However
it was observed that in this case it is the couple itself who were used to wash in different cycle
modes and therefore they could compare. In this case it is not clear if the system lead occupants to
explore a variety of options.
Some questions were carried out lastly to study the experience of the participants of this expe-
riment. They answered that "It’s interesting to see what’s happening and it’s an incentive to make
changes." when they were asked if they found that the system facilitates the changes. They also
added that they felt involved and interested in the issues and the changes. They believe that this
type of system could be useful to residents "In order to save money and have a more controlled,
more reasonable consumption.", they said. In terms of the process of annotation manually, they
mentioned that it was not a complicated task as such, but they could easily forget to do it. They
proposed that an app could be better, but it will still be important to not forget.
In the process of annotation, it was noticed that there were some confusions from the occu-
pants. The researchers expected the annotations to be carried out differently in some cases. For
instance, in the case of the washing machine, considering that an automated system will recover
its data, it was mainly expected in the column of objects implicated that the users will annotate the
type of clothes to wash (information that cannot be recovered with sensors). But this information
was added in the intention column. It is true that there are not many options of intentions when wa-
shing. It is expected that the intention is "to wash" or "to clean". The researchers conclude that it is
therefore very important to explain what it is expected for each of the annotations to make sure the
occupants understood correctly. Maybe in this case, using a paper format generated this confusion,
since there was no automated system to program the experiment and indicate the appliances im-
plicated in the experiment. Though it is correct that is an appliance used for the fact studied. Let’s
remember that when programming the experiments, the inhabitant should select the appliances
used, directly in the interface. In this way it will be automatically recorded the appliances used in
an experiment.
Lastly, it was observed that human errors were present as well. For example, there is no wa-
shing program of 40 minutes, but the occupant should have made a mistake and wrote "40 min".
When automatizing the learning process in the system, it will consider the annotations "40 min"
and "eco-40°C" as 2 different modalities, even when for the inhabitants could represent the same
modality. For this reason, in the interface, it will be important to show the occupants the previous
options they have registered in order that they can refer to one same annotation, using the same
name.

Experiment 2 in site "gamma" : How much energy do I consume when I use the dishwasher in
different wash programs and in different contexts ?

The inhabitant provided beginning and end date and time each time the fact was carried out.
The Where ? question was always responded in the same way, "kitchen". The Why ? question
(intention) was registered with two main labels : "washing (departure WE)" and "washing". The
How ?, question was declared as "half-load" cycle or "full load" cycle. In Who ? question (partici-
pants involved), the inhabitant declared "2" to define that 2 people participated and lastly, in the
objects implicated, it was annotated "dishwasher". The performance was mainly annotated as "4"
and "3" only in two occasions.
In this case, it was interesting to observe that the inhabitants annotated as modality if the dish-
washer was used as a full load or half-load. The inhabitant mentioned that this is not a program to
choose from in the dishwasher, the program that the inhabitant used always was 60°C. Differently
from the experiment in site "kappa", where when using the washing machine they configured the
machine in different washing programs often.
The information about how full is the charge is not possible to recover with sensors. Only the
inhabitant can know the amount of dishes introduced to the dishwasher, and it is also true that

programming a full washing cycle to wash few dishes might represent a wasteful behaviour.
It was also mentioned by the occupants that another option is the "eco" program, but that it
usually takes longer time to end the cycle. In the "eco" mode, they believe the temperature of the
water should be lower, but then to compensate and have a good quality washing, the cycle is longer.
It could be interesting to measure the energy consumption and evaluate if there is a difference
between this configuration and the configuration washing at 60°C. Might it consume less energy
even if the cycle is longer ? Would the occupants be interested in changing their behaviour ?
It was also confirmed that for certain activities, such as cooking, washing and eating the in-
tention could be obvious : cooking, washing and eating, not as in the case of opening the window
in which there are at least two intentions "refresh" and "ventilate". None the less, the inhabitants
seemed to justify the fact that they washed "half load" because of "weekend departure".
In the objects implied, it was annotated the appliance used to wash the dishes. But no infor-
mation was provided of the state on the objects to be washed. If this information was annotated, it
is believed that it could provide important information to evaluate the performance. For example,
if "greasy dishes" are washed at a low temperature, maybe the washing quality is not enough.
Lastly, observations were made on the fact that the annotations system allowed the inhabitants
to discover about their habits. They could then try, in new experiments, different washing programs
for different situations. This could help them compare and learn how to be more careful with the
energy consumption.

#### 8.1.3 Activities impact experiments

Experiment 3 in site "gamma" : How much energy do I waste when the computer is left on and
I’m not working ?
This experiment had to be performed twice due to an issue in the sensors (it is important to
mention this and the reader will next understand why). The first time the experiment was deve-
loped, the duration of the experiment was of 3 weeks from the 15th of april to the 7th of may.
During this time, the occupant forgot to turn off the computer 6 times. The longest time that the
computer was left on were 3h. The inhabitant declared the place as the "living room" in all cases.
The intention was always annotated as "oversight". Only one participant was annotated in the im-
plied participants section and in implied objects were declared only the computer. It is important
to mention that the fact was not annotated in the beginning of the usage of the computer, but when
the occupant realised that the computer was left on, then the inhabitant estimated the moment in
which she had stopped using the computer to declare the moment in which the "oversight" could
be considered to have started.
In this period of annotations, one of the sensors (the computer power plug) failed. Therefore it
was decided to carry out the experiment again. In the second time, the inhabitant provided only one
label. The occupant explained that in the first experiment, she realised that she forgot sometimes
to turn off the computer, so for the second experiment, she payed more attention to actually turn
off the computer when leaving the desk. She said she was sorry about the fact that there were not
many annotations in the second experiment, but she did not want to leave the computer On, on
purpose. It can be concluded from this situation that the inhabitant became more conscious of a
wasteful behaviour by annotating the fact and then, the inhabitant decided on its own to change
and have a more sober behaviour.

Experiment 4 in site "gamma" : Does the energy consumption of the fridge increases when I
go shopping and put food inside the fridge ?
The experiment about the fridge energy consumption is an experiment in which the perfor-
mance is not evaluated by the inhabitant, but the rest of the characterizations of the fact are re-
quested. The start and end of the fact were provided, the maximum duration of the fact was of 10

minutes, while the minimum 5 minutes. This makes sense, since the main fact characterized is the
one of introducing food to the fridge. The Where ? question, of the fact was always answered as :
"the kitchen". The Why ? question was annotated as "groceries storage". In the How ? question, no
annotation was provided. For the Who ? question (participants involved), it was written 1 and in
the What ? question (objects implied), it was written "fridge".
The intention was well described, given that other motivations can result in the use of the
fridge such as "cooking", "checking what is there to eat", "doing the list to do groceries", etc.
As observed, the How ? question in this case was not easy to answer, given that it should be
mainly opening the door of the fridge. Maybe, if the fridge includes two different doors, one for
the fridge and one for the freezer, a modality could have been written, but it is not evident that this
distinction should be made. However, to perform a "groceries storage" in the fridge, there is just
one way : opening the door, therefore, probably no need to be more specific.
In the objects implied, as it was just mentioned, in a connected system, it would be known that
the appliances implicated was the fridge. But in this case, the annotation was provided manually.
For this reason, the occupant might consider important to define what appliance is used. However
let’s remember that the idea of adding annotations is to recover information that cannot be recove-
red by sensors, then, in the objects implied it could have been written "food" (in general), "frozen
food", "christmas turkey", etc.

### 8.2 Discussion from site "kappa" and site "gamma" and the use of

semantic annotations
The characterisation of facts was tested in two different sites. Annotations were carried out by
the residents for specific experiment questions. In both sites, there were two common experiment
questions, one related with the use of windows and another one related with washing machines (in
the site "kappa", the clothes washing machine was studied and in site "gamma", the dishwasher).
The annotations were different in both sites. There was no restriction in the annotations. The
tests were carried out explaining to the inhabitants that the annotations were free and they could
answer to each of the characterizations as they wished.
In this section we were interested in studying the next questions. Here presented some brief
responses.
The first question addressed was whether annotating improves understanding of the human
behaviour impacts. The results confirmed that specially once the inhabitants had access to the
energy consumption measurements, for instance in the experiment about the washing machine
of site kappa, the inhabitants realised that washing at 60°C was taking too much time and was
the option that consumed more energy. Thanks to the annotations they could associate a washing
program with its impacts.
The second question examined whether residents feel interested and involved in making an-
notations. The residents of the kappa site, mentioned that it was interesting to be able to observe
for example, if washing in eco-mode, actually helps reducing energy consumption. In terms of the
process of annotation manually, they mentioned that it was not a complicated task, but it was easy
to forget to annotate. They proposed that an app could be better, but it will still be important to not
forget. In the gamma site, the inhabitant mentioned as well that annotating was not difficult but
like burdensome or tedious.
The third question considered if residents felt interested and involved making changes in their
behaviour. Interviews in the kappa site confirmed interest in making changes. They also added
that they felt involved and interested.The experiments could help the inhabitants realising, not just
about that they performed an activity, but how they performed it. In the "kappa" site, for the expe-
riment using the washing machine, the inhabitants could associate a configuration of the machine
(30°C, 60°C, 15", etc.) with a specific impact, not only in terms of energy, but also in terms of
time. They were actually considering making some behaviour changes, by preferring to wash at
30°C to reduce their energy consumption. In the "gamma" site, the behaviour of the inhabitant
actually changed after the first experiment measuring the energy waste while the computer was
on but no presence was detected. The inhabitant became conscious of turning off the computer,
before leaving the desk.
The fourth question intended to answer if annotating enables flexibility to be assessed in terms
of the changes to be made. In the kappa site, they could compare the different washing options
they used and their impacts, which could help then choosing to make some changes. In the gamma
site, their modalities were less diverse. It was observed by analysing the results in this part that the
inhabitants mainly responded to how they carry out their activities, currently. It can be interesting
to develop a feature in the system that could invite the inhabitants to explore different options.
Occupants could test the different possibilities and measure the effects. By registering measu-
rement’s information of the effects and grading their satisfaction level, they could decide on the
changes to make.
In the fifth question we were interested in knowing what annotations are provided if inhabi-
tants are told that there is no restriction on what they can annotate, but they should follow the
semantic (5WH+performance) as a guide. In the kappa site occupants faced annotation confusion,
misplacing clothing types under "intention" instead of "objects implicated." Paper-based recording
may have contributed, as a digital interface could automate appliance selection. Human errors, like
incorrect cycle names, suggest the need for an interface that suggests previously used options to

ensure consistency.
Also in the site gamma, the inhabitants recorded the dishwasher’s load size (full or half) as a
modality, though they clarified that the dishwasher only offers a fixed 60°C program, not load-size
options. The load size could also be expected to be provided by answering the question what ?
(objects involved) in the facts to analyse. In the objects implied, it was annotated the appliance
used to wash the dishes. But no information was provided of the state on the objects to be washed.
In site gamma for the fridge experiment since the annotation was done manually, the occupant
specified the appliance used, by answering to the question "What ?". However, annotations should
capture information sensors can’t detect. Therefore, listing objects like "veggies" or "frozen food"
could be interesting.
It is considered important to show specific examples of possible annotations in the characte-
rization of different facts in order to better guide the occupants on what could be interesting to
annotate (although this aspect would need to be developed also by analysing other experiment
answers). It was observed that in some cases no annotations were provided, while in other cases,
unnecessary information was added. For example, we could show that if there is a sensor connec-
ted to an object, we don’t need to label it because it will be automatically identified during the
experiment.
When designing the interface, it will be useful to introduce the system to the inhabitants by
showing predefined examples that can guide them before they can develop their own experiments,
as shown in Annex B.
The sixth question is about the possibility of context be captured for each fact by using an-
notations. In both sites it can be observed a better comprehension of the facts carried out, given
to the details provided by the annotations. Different intentions were observed, for instance, in the
annotations of the window and the washing machine experiment in the kappa site. In all the ex-
periments we could observe who carried out the facts or how many people were involved by the
specification of the participants. The place in which the fact happened was always clearly pro-
vided. By annotating manually the start and end of the facts, the duration was well stated. The
preferences of the inhabitants could also be deduced by observing the performance annotations.
Some clarification can be made on what could be annotated in labels such as the intention
(question why ?), the objects implied (question what ?)and the modalities (question how ?). In some
cases, these labels did not make sense or could seem difficult to define them for the inhabitants.
As it was seen in the previous and in the next question.
As 7th question it was wondered if there could be annotations left empty. In some cases, anno-
tations were not provided, as for instance the How ? question in Site "gamma" for the experiment
of computer. It is comprehensible that this was left empty, given that it was the fact of leaving the
computer on, so no specific details could be added about it.
Answering "How ?" for the fridge experiment was also not evident, as it mainly involved ope-
ning the fridge door. If the fridge had separate doors for the fridge and freezer, specifying a door
could be relevant, but the inhabitant decided not to annotate this column.
However, they were blank annotations that made sense. No annotations were left empty for
reasons such lack of will/interest from the inhabitants.
Finally it was addressed if it is interesting for the inhabitants to assess human behaviour and
their impact on energy consumption. The dwelling studied seemed to have interest in reducing their
impact. In the kappa site, the occupants answered that "It’s interesting to see what’s happening and
it’s an incentive to make changes." They added "I’ve realized that I’m used of washing in "eco"
mode, but does it really consume less energy that other options ? Now I can see the real energy
consumption, because it’s measured." In the gamma site, actions were immediate, to avoid leaving
the computer on when nobody was using it, this can show the interest of the inhabitant in this
subjects.

### 8.3 Chapter conclusions

In summary, the study demonstrated that annotations enhance understanding and interest in
making changes. The annotations could better capture the context of the facts. The preferences
of the inhabitants could be inferred by observing the performance annotations. However in some
cases, some annotations were considered irrelevant by the inhabitants for the experiment and were
left empty.
Showing examples to inhabitants of possible annotations can be useful for them to understand
the fact characterization and reduce misunderstandings or confusions.
Future work should focus on simplifying the annotation process through digital interfaces and
enabling dynamic feedback to guide residents in exploring diverse behavioral scenarios.
In these tests, the annotations primarily highlighted the habits of the inhabitants. This approach
allows for an initial observation of the causes (behaviors) as they usually occur, helping the inhabi-
tants to evaluate the current effects. It could be interesting to add a feature that invites inhabitants
to test other options so that the inhabitants can compare and make more informed decisions.

to the annotations aiding system approach to simulate the Interactive and Cooperative learning
process using the recovered annotations.
